Ostia tenebrosa: A Tractate on Will

Litterae tenebrarum, Volume 1

ISBN: 9798237163520 / 978-8384679883

In November 1516, a house near the Market Square in Mainz was broken open after its master had disappeared. On the table lay a manuscript; on the floor, nine engraved sheets arranged in a square. Two theologians condemned the text as blasphemous. The Inquisitor ordered it burned but first had a copy made.

This is that copy.

Ostia tenebrosa is a tractate on the will, written from the darkness against which the preachers warned. Its eleven chapters lead through temptation, blackness, the white desert where Nobody walks, the birth of a Name, encounters with other wills, the descent into hell, and an ascent without a summit.

Its teachings unfold through paradoxes and strange accounts: a pig tried for murder, an exorcist who hears his forgotten name, a guard who asks the judges to punish his dice. Between the chapters, nine figures reveal fragments of the vanished author's life — a wine merchant's son who squandered his inheritance and met someone beside the Rhine.

The book offers a path that cannot be followed by obedience.

Glossa tenebrosa: A Diary on Holding

Litterae tenebrarum, Volume 2

ISBN: 9798237204674 / 978-8384679906

Paris, autumn 1888. A Russian physician studying at Charcot's clinic begins a notebook on a Latin treatise written in 1516. Reading Ostia tenebrosa through the lens of medicine, he sets its account of will, darkness, and the Name against his clinical experience.

The second volume of Litterae tenebrarum, Glossa tenebrosa returns to the earlier tractate through the diary of its reader: a physician who intends to explain its author, even as the manuscript becomes part of his own life.

Rima tenebrarum: A Notebook on Encounter

Litterae tenebrarum, Volume 3

ISBN: 9798237188691

Paris, 1944–1947. The archivist of the Russian cathedral on the rue Daru, who checked lists of baptized persons for the German Kommandantur, keeps the doctor's notebooks from Glossa tenebrosa. He carves anew the nine burned woodcuts of Ostia, and a second hand begins writing in his notebook. A continuation of Glossa: a book of short chapters on how one will encounters another.
