Not/One: Philosophy of Freedom

Volume IX of Philosophy of the Left-Hand Path

The book takes the academic dispute over free will into ontology. Freedom is located in the act that closes a deliberation — a settling following neither from the reasons weighed nor from the prior state. Law, nature, and matter are read as determinations instituted and carried forward in repetition. Not/One names the break in which inherited determinacy ceases and another cut can be born. The volume stands on its own and does not presuppose the previous eight.

Volume IX

Not/One: Philosophy of Freedom

The book does not prove freedom and does not set out to. It demarcates the free act, states the primitive that authorship requires, separates its position from agent-causal and non-causal accounts, keeps authorship local to particular acts, and builds identity across them through the Name. Determinism terminates in a primitive of its own. The resulting symmetry leaves two wagers on the table and asks what follows from taking either one.

Contents

  1. Chapter 1John stands before money he could steal. Hunger, prohibition, fear, and calculation can each be reconsidered, and reflection permits another consideration to be added without limit. Yet an action requires the examination to end. The decisive operation is the settling: the closing that establishes an answer as sufficient. Freedom, wherever it occurs, belongs to this operation rather than to the grounds preceding it.
  2. Chapter 2Determinism locates the settling in character, upbringing, inherited standards, or the architecture assigning weight to reasons. Indeterminism seems to leave only chance. Both readings concern where the outcome derives from. A third asks by whom the question was settled. On this reading the answer is posited in the act, and its author arises with it.
  3. Chapter 3A law states that B follows A without carrying out the passage from A to B. Turning laws into premises generates a regress inside inference; treating them as powers leaves production primitive. Frank's potential ground relocates the same problem into development. Determinism can describe and predict the transition while leaving the transition itself underived.
  4. Chapter 4The regress belongs to argument rather than to nature, leaving the law intact and the question of production still open. A Humean account can reduce causation to regular arrangement; a powers account can take productive necessity as basic. Mechanisms decompose transitions into smaller measurable ones and eventually stop at a force, disposition, transfer, or production. Causation and freedom therefore terminate in primitives alike.
  5. Chapter 5Necessity or production never appears directly among the things observed. Laws, mechanisms, interventions, statistical distributions, capacities, and causal processes make events intelligible through structures stable beyond the single case. Singular causation remains possible, yet its causal status becomes knowable through an established form. Reproducibility performs the epistemic work while production remains unseen.
  6. Chapter 6Determinism requires the complete state of John and the world to permit a single continuation. Such a complete state occurs once. Causal knowledge reaches it only through types and models constructed by discarding detail, while the detail needed to fix this particular outcome destroys repeatability. The certified description yields tendencies and rates; the description claimed to determine John cannot itself be independently certified.
  7. Chapter 7A hundred similar Johns can steal or refuse while retrospective explanations remain available for either result. Hidden differences may genuinely determine them, though they become explanations only when identified independently of the outcomes. Strong determinism ultimately becomes a wager on a complete calculation available in principle and inaccessible in practice. Even the act of defending that wager presupposes judgments whose status its own account places in question.
  8. Chapter 8Chance covers several different things: ignorance of determining conditions, the accidental crossing of causal lines, and a genuinely underived event. Properties borrowed from the first two senses are too easily transferred to the third. An absence of sufficient antecedent cause does not establish absence of authorship. Freedom and pure chance share underivation; their difference concerns whether an answer belongs to anyone.
  9. Chapter 9Intention, bodily origin, agreement with motives, the feeling of agency, and later endorsement all fail to distinguish an authored act from an event occurring within a person. Before the settling there is a bearer: organism, memory, personality, and available grounds. The author of this particular answer arises in the answering. Authorship is the primitive internal form of the act.
  10. Chapter 10Each free settling constitutes an author locally, while body, memory, habit, and biography preserve the bearer across time. They do not by themselves unite the authors of separate settlings. The Name is another free act that takes those authors as one without deriving their unity from a common nature or dictating later conduct. A life can therefore contain free acts without ever instituting one author across them.
  11. Chapter 11Agent-causal theories place a finished substance behind the act; non-causal theories locate activity within the event; compatibilism attributes action through an already formed character. Fichte's Tathandlung supplies another structure: act, instituted relation, and author arise together. The bearer contributes the material and available grounds, while the settling gives them the form of someone's answer. Contemporary accounts of constitution, practical knowledge, and question-settling clarify parts of this structure without establishing libertarian sourcehood.
  12. Chapter 12The settling occurs in John's brain and body. Physical realization is therefore accepted while strong causal closure — sufficient antecedent physical determination of every outcome — is rejected. The free act adds no second force beside the neural event; it is the same transition understood as the resolution of an open practical question. Physical indeterminacy supplies room for such openness, while authorship remains irreducible to quantum chance, non-computability, or downward constraint.
  13. Chapter 13Neural preparation, unpredictability, effort, complexity, practical knowledge, and the experience of choosing can accompany determined and free action alike. Libet and later decoding experiments identify preparation without establishing sufficient determination of the eventual choice. Neither observation nor introspection reveals whether the settling was underived. Freedom therefore enters as a postulate governing how the act is taken, analogous to an axiom that cannot be proved from within the system it helps organize.
  14. Chapter 14The postulate of freedom has positive content: a question stood open, a ground was established as sufficient, and an author arose in giving the answer. Kant supplies the nearest precedent, though the act here remains inside the physical world. Scientific theory choice exhibits a parallel structure in which reasons bear without algorithmically compelling a conclusion. Determinism and freedom emerge as rival wagers, backed respectively by predictive practice and imputation.
  15. Chapter 15A free settling leaves a postulate behind, and what was once settled can later answer automatically. Habituation turns acts into character; a freely instituted rule becomes a source of unfreedom when it acquires the authority of necessity. Lehavah names the relation in which what was laid down remains revocable. Practice can train reflection and reopening, though no technique can manufacture the next free settling.
  16. Chapter 16Epistemic uncertainty, physical indeterminacy, and agential openness are three different gaps. Freedom concerns the third: prior grounds fail to establish their own sufficiency, and the settling resolves the question as someone's answer. The agential gap is no empty region waiting before the act. It is constituted in the underived resolution itself, which closes the joint and leaves behind a determination capable of entering later repetition.
  17. Chapter 17Causality becomes the primitive of repetition and freedom the primitive of birth. Repetition carries a form already in force; birth institutes determinacy that no prior form supplied. Self-positing brings an authoring side and a posited side into determination together. Continued execution becomes sediment, then law and nature; the posited side persisting in repetition becomes matter. Freedom and unfreedom are therefore two states of the same instituted determination.
  18. Chapter 18The settling does not require a pre-existing temporal container for its instituting aspect. A cut establishes its own before and after, while sediment carries that asymmetry forward as duration. Repetition makes world-time possible and gives separate wills something enduring to encounter. Whitehead, Deleuze, Simondon, Schelling, Husserl, and Derrida approach pieces of this structure from different directions. A human act institutes a local boundary that enters the already sedimented order as another stubborn fact.
  19. Chapter 19The oldest recorded term for freedom, Sumerian ama-gi₄, names cancellation and return: debt bondage is revoked and the debtor returns home. Other linguistic traditions move among release, belonging, free birth, self-rule, and self-origin, often turning an act or relation into an abstract condition possessed by someone. The history of the vocabulary mirrors sedimentation itself: verbs and relations harden into nouns and natures.
  20. Chapter 20Replacing freedom with a verb does not solve the problem, since processes too can acquire repeatable natures. Apophasis clears false names and risks turning the resulting absence into a sacred substance. Pointing directs attention toward the act without claiming to define it; silence stops where another word would falsify. Proper names approach singularity only while they are performed as address. Philosophies of the Whole erase the seam by absorbing agent and act into unity.
  21. Chapter 21Every word cuts a field into what it names and what it excludes. The differentiating operation cannot appear among the differentiated results without becoming another result itself. Freedom and grasping meet at the birth of a cut, where a distinction begins to operate without an earlier instance of that same distinction supplying it. Language belongs to the duration after the cut and therefore reaches its source structurally late.
  22. Chapter 22Nāgārjuna exhausts logical positions while Derrida dissolves presence into difference and deferral. Both operate with distinctions already available. A new cut institutes sides, temporal asymmetry, and eventually the trace by which a distinction can be drawn again. Thermostats and systems respond according to lines already supplied. Freedom concerns the institution of the line that makes a difference count.
  23. Chapter 23Tiamat names what the cut is born from without turning that source into a thing, chaos, emptiness, or hidden domain. Marduk's stroke becomes the mythic image of differentiation. Not/One uses the slash to hold open the interval before unity, plurality, assertion, and negation have become established operations. Language can approach the interval only by displaying its own failure to contain it.
  24. Chapter 24A purely negative account would quietly turn Not/One into a primal substance or apophatic god. Tiamat is therefore stated positively as the instituting break in which inherited determinacy ceases and new determinacy has not yet taken form. Castoriadis's magma and Cusanus's possest approach the same boundary while leaving institution anonymous or absorbing possibility into fullness. Tiamat is openness in the event; freedom is authored resolution; Marduk is the cut once settled.
  25. Chapter 25Another freedom appears when another's act breaks the image held of him and no replacement image immediately closes the opening. The encounter is enactive and pre-logical: the other is held as the author of a singular deed rather than subsumed under a type. Doctrines of unity erase the separation necessary for genuine encounter. Relations nevertheless require sediment, provided their accumulated history remains something the wills hold rather than something that commands them.
  26. Chapter 26The move from human freedom to cosmology requires an explicit wager: physical forms are cuts instituted and retained. The chapter asks what it means for a distinction to remain a distinction. A mechanism can respond to a line already given, while holding a difference requires its sides to remain marked against each other; at the thinnest level this marking is willing. The result is a pluralist panvoluntarism in which instituted distinctions can harden into natural law. The demiurge is world-order whose authoring side has frozen into its own execution, continuously dying as positing becomes nature.
  27. Chapter 27Matter is the maximum hardening of a cut rather than a second substance opposed to spirit. Sediment supplies the soil through which acts appear; opacity begins when what was instituted presents itself as an eternal source. Tiamat names chaos at the birth of determination, while the meon names chaos at its exhaustion into indistinguishability. Entropy supplies a physical image of this descent. The usual problem of life emerging from dead matter is inverted into the hardening of the living into the dead.
  28. Chapter 28Nabokov's Cincinnatus is condemned for opacity in a society organized around complete legibility. Universal causality becomes a demand that every future act be readable from the predicates of its bearer. Transparent sediment reveals its own instituted seam; complete legibility means that the living has been exhausted by its profile. Institutions naturally reward readability because prediction depends on stable types. Cincinnatus exposes the deterministic ideal as a completed world in which no clearing remains.
  29. Chapter 29Ama-gi₄ returns as freedom understood through cancellation. Withdrawal moves from an execution presented as necessity back toward the settling concealed within it, allowing the cut to become drawable again. The mother is no lost original state or secure ground; she is the opening that occurs when sediment loses its authority. Withdrawal itself cannot become a program, since automatic cancellation would merely establish another rule.
  30. Chapter 30Practice addresses five forms of acquired determination: belief, body, prohibition, purpose, and author. Pyrrhonian suspension, Socratic examination, bodily inhibition, voluntary discomfort, transgression, non-teleological practice, self-inquiry, Chöd, and koan work expose grounds that had been deciding in advance. Each practice has a counterfeit in which liberation hardens into another compulsion or identity. Technique can suspend authority. The settling that may follow remains outside anything technique can produce.
  31. Chapter 31Freedom and damage can look identical because both interrupt automatic execution. Their difference appears when another answer is required. Depersonalization can damage ownership of thought; dissociation can silence bodily signals; compulsive transgression can destroy the boundary it needed; depression can remove the capacity to posit a direction; radical ego-dissolution can leave no author able to arise. An interval preserves the capacity to answer. A ruin destroys it.
  32. AfterwordGi is the reed cut into the stylus that inscribes a distinction in clay, while its hollow can also carry wind and turn breath into voice. Tiamat mothers the birth of the cut; Lilith keeps the passage open so that the Name can speak again. Gi₄ is return: the hardened inscription becomes withdrawable and the cut returns to the living interval of its birth. Reed, wind, and return gather the book's final image of freedom — a form that can hold without becoming mute, and can be taken back before its sediment acquires the authority of nature.
